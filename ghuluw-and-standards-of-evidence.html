Ghuluw, Miracles and the Burden of Proof

Separating theological impossibility from historical uncertainty in reports about the Imams.

Editorial draft28 October 2026•6 min read•Theology

Terms must be defined

In Imāmī theology, ghuluw describes the transgression of appropriate limits in beliefs about the Prophet and the Imams. Classical discussions of tafwīḍ and the attribution of divine prerogatives make it important to define precisely what a disputed narration claims.1

A report suggesting independent divine authority makes a different theological assertion from one describing a miracle undertaken only by Allah’s permission. Yet distinguishing the claims conceptually does not automatically validate the historical report containing either one.

What al-Ṣadūq establishes

Al-Ṣadūq’s al-Iʿtiqādāt includes an explicit condemnation of the ghulāt and mufawwiḍa. That should be taken seriously as part of the classical Imāmī tradition, not treated as a modern innovation.2

At the same time, one should not create a false binary between rejecting independent divinity and rejecting every report of an extraordinary event. The doctrinal judgment and the historical authentication must be argued separately.

Qurʾānic possibility and historical attribution

The Qurʾān attributes extraordinary acts to the Prophet ʿĪsā (ع), explicitly “by Allah’s permission” (3:49), and describes the bringing of the throne to Sulaymān (ع) by one who possessed knowledge of the Book (27:40). These texts establish that an extraordinary occurrence is not inherently a claim to independent divinity.

But they cannot by themselves certify a particular later report about an Imam. One still has to demonstrate that the Imam was reliably reported to have said or done the act in question.

Two separate tests

Doctrinal coherence: Is the claim compatible with monotheism? Historical authenticity: Is there sufficient evidence that the particular claim originated from the attributed source?

A disciplined conclusion

Rebuttals should resist both reflexive dismissal and easy credulity. If a narration is rejected, identify whether the problem is its chain, book provenance, contradiction with established doctrine, textual instability or another specific defect.

Editorial status: research note.This is a methodological primer; it is not an authentication of any specific miracle narration or a substitute for a detailed comparison of al-Ṣadūq, al-Mufīd and later scholars.
