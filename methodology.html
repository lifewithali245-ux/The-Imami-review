Our research practice

Methodology, not slogans.

These standards guide our analysis of contested reports and scholarly statements. They are commitments for every future publication—not a claim that every draft is already complete.

Editorial standards

Any article intended as a rebuttal should cite the original claim accurately, explain what it can fairly establish, and specify which additional conclusions require independent evidence.

01

Source before quotation

Locate the primary source, the edition, page or report number; do not attribute a sentence from a secondary paraphrase.

02

Context before verdict

Read what comes before and after the quotation, and consider whether the author distinguishes possible, probable and proven.

03

Transmission and text

Examine isnād, ṭabaqāt, book provenance, manuscript recensions, independent corroboration and matn criticism.

04

Strength of conclusion

Separate unknown, weak, contradicted and positively fabricated reports. State scholarly disagreements fairly.

A model for the individual report

  1. Transcribe the matn and record the edition, variants, and bibliographic witness.
  2. Record each available isnād, including disputed narrators and chronological constraints.
  3. Determine whether the chain transmits an earlier written source or a specific recension.
  4. Compare parallel narrations and identify whether apparent corroborations are genuinely independent.
  5. Review the Qurʾānic and established Sunnah context, and distinguish contradiction from qualification.
  6. Quote the assessments of named scholars from their own works; distinguish their principles from conclusions attributed by others.
  7. Reach a calibrated conclusion and acknowledge remaining uncertainty.

Editorial integrity

Draft essays are clearly labelled. Translations are identified as editorial unless they come from a cited published translation. A critical response to another website is not affiliated with or endorsed by that website. Corrections should be made openly when primary evidence requires them.

We do not present scholarly disagreements as a substitute for demonstrating the reasoning behind each position.
