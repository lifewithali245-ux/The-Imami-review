Uṣūl al-Fiqh · Research Note 03

What Does It Mean to Test Ḥadīth Against the Qurʾān?

Agreement, conflict, specification and the interpretation of reports of ʿarḍ ʿalā al-kitāb.

Editorial draft28 October 2026•7 min read•Qurʾān & Sunnah

The instruction and the interpretative issue

Al-Kāfī includes a chapter entitled “Following the Sunnah and Evidence of the Book”, containing reports that direct believers to measure narrations against the Qurʾān.1

The question is not whether the Qurʾān is authoritative. It is what constitutes contradiction, and whether the absence of an explicit Qurʾānic parallel necessarily defeats a report about matters the Qurʾān treats only in general terms.

A well-known text

كُلُّ شَيْءٍ مَرْدُودٌ إِلَى الْكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةِ وَكُلُّ حَدِيثٍ لَا يُوَافِقُ كِتَابَ اللهِ فَهُوَ زُخْرُفٌ

“Everything is referred back to the Book and the Sunnah; any narration that does not accord with the Book of Allah is embellishment.”

Al-Kāfī · vol. 1, book 2, chapter 22, report 3 · editorial translation

This report is among those given a ṣaḥīḥ grading by al-Majlisī in the grading reproduced alongside the online chapter text. Such judgments should still be read with reference to their grading system and manuscript edition.2

Contradiction versus explanation

Suppose a text establishes an obligation in general terms, while a report supplies a detail concerning how it is performed. The second text is not automatically in conflict with the first simply because the detail was not explicitly stated in the general text.

Conflict

A report asserts what cannot be reconciled with an established, decisive scriptural proposition.

Explication

A report offers a detail or qualification compatible with a general scriptural command.

The task is to explain, with clear uṣūlī reasoning, how to distinguish the two—and to avoid deciding a report’s authenticity only because its contents feel familiar or unfamiliar.

Toward a cumulative reading

Sound analysis combines linguistic interpretation, evidence of transmission, corroborating material and the wider legal or theological context. These tools can narrow apparent contradictions and also expose genuine ones.

The report of Yūnus b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān discussed in our lead article is notable because it explicitly mentions earlier narrated material as a possible corroborating witness, in addition to the Qurʾān and Sunnah.3
